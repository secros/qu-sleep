Sleep Calculator — FAQ

What is a sleep cycle and why does it last 90 minutes?
A sleep cycle is a repeating pattern of sleep stages: light sleep (N1, N2), deep sleep (N3, also called slow-wave sleep), and REM (rapid eye movement) sleep. Each full cycle takes roughly 90 minutes on average, though this can vary from 80–120 minutes person to person and can lengthen across the night. Waking at the end of a cycle — rather than in the middle of deep or REM sleep — means you surface during the lightest stage, leaving you alert rather than groggy.
How many sleep cycles do I need?
Most adults need 5–6 complete cycles per night, equating to 7.5–9 hours of sleep. Teenagers and children typically need more. 4 cycles (6 hrs) is a bare minimum that many can sustain short-term but is insufficient long-term. The National Sleep Foundation recommends 7–9 hours for adults aged 18–64, and 7–8 hours for adults 65+.
What is the 14-minute "fall asleep" factor?
Sleep onset latency — how long it takes you to actually fall asleep after lying down — averages around 10–20 minutes for healthy sleepers. This calculator uses 14 minutes as the default, which represents a healthy average. You can uncheck this option if you fall asleep unusually fast (under 5 minutes, which can indicate sleep deprivation) or want to calculate from the exact moment you're actually asleep.
What is the best time to wake up?
The "best" wake time is whatever aligns with the end of a complete 90-minute cycle. Beyond that, your body has a natural preference called a chronotype — morning larks naturally peak early, night owls later. Waking consistently at the same time each day (even weekends) is one of the most powerful things you can do for sleep quality, as it anchors your circadian rhythm.
Why do I feel groggy even after 8 hours of sleep?
Grogginess upon waking — called sleep inertia — usually means your alarm interrupted a deep sleep or REM stage rather than a light stage. By timing your wake-up to land at the end of a cycle, you dramatically reduce this effect. Other causes include poor sleep quality (sleep apnea, frequent waking), inconsistent sleep schedules, or sleeping in an environment that's too warm.
What time should I go to bed for a 5am, 6am, or 7am wake up?
For a 5:00 AM wake-up (5 cycles): go to bed at 9:16 PM. For a 6:00 AM wake-up: go to bed at 10:16 PM (5 cycles) or 8:46 PM (6 cycles). For a 7:00 AM wake-up: go to bed at 11:16 PM (5 cycles) or 9:46 PM (6 cycles). These include 14 minutes to fall asleep.
Is a 20-minute nap actually effective?
Yes — a 10–20 minute "power nap" stays in the light sleep stages (N1/N2), giving you a boost without sleep inertia. A 90-minute nap completes a full cycle and includes REM, which is restorative but requires more time. Avoid naps over 30 minutes unless you're doing a full 90-minute cycle, as you risk waking mid-deep-sleep and feeling worse.
